### Advancing AI fleet reliability through industry collaboration

<p>Customers depend on reliable infrastructure to run workloads at scale. As AI infrastructure continues to expand, ensuring fleet capacity, availability, and reliability is more important than ever.</p>
<p>Large-scale, multi-vendor AI clusters introduce significant operational complexity. Accelerators, CPUs, memory, storage, networking, and management controllers each bring distinct reliability challenges and workflows. Fragmented vendor tools and processes can delay fault isolation, extend recovery times, and increase the risk of unresolved issues returning to production.</p>
<p>Open standards help address this complexity. Through the Open Compute Project (OCP), Microsoft is collaborating with AMD, Arm, Google, Intel, Meta, NVIDIA, and others to develop and drive industry standards for reliability, availability, and serviceability (RAS), debugging, diagnostics, manageability, and firmware updates.</p>
<p>These efforts support two connected goals: raising the industry bar by embedding reliability into Microsoft and partner platforms from the outset and establishing consistent platform signals and fleet insights to support increasingly AI-assisted fleet operations. Together, they enable broader hardware support and proactive operations—preventing failures, predicting issues, and automating recovery where feasible,&nbsp;helping keep customer workloads running with less complexity.&nbsp;</p>
<p>As shown in below figure, OCP standards, platform integration, and compliance validation help reduce multi-vendor complexity. Fleet telemetry and insights enable AI-assisted prevention, prediction, and repair—advancing toward a self-managed AI fleet.</p>
<img />
<p><strong>Containing faults while protecting healthy workloads</strong></p>
<p>Common RAS requirements establish standardized approaches for detecting, reporting, containing, and recovering from hardware and platform errors. The objective is to respond at the smallest safe scope possible by isolating an affected component or resource rather than unnecessarily resetting an entire device, node, rack, or cluster. Effective fault containment minimizes service disruption, protects data integrity, and preserves healthy workloads.</p>
<p>At rack scale, failures can often span multiple domains, including compute, networking, power, and cooling infrastructure. Emerging reliability standards emphasize correlated event analysis and shared error information to help distinguish root causes from downstream symptoms and support coordinated recovery actions.</p>
<p><strong>Turning diagnostic results into restored capacity&nbsp;</strong></p>
<p>Once a fault is contained, the next priority is to repair the system and restore capacity. Standardized crash capture, debug data collection, diagnostics, and service workflows help identify root causes and guide repair decisions. Effective diagnostics must do more than run quickly: they must identify the right service action to resolve the issue and return the system to production. Compliance and validation tools help identify requirement gaps, validate interoperability, and assess readiness for reliable fleet-scale deployment.</p>
<p><strong>Improving fleet manageability</strong></p>
<p>GPU manageability standards improve fleet observability and operational readiness. Optimized telemetry streaming reduces operational overhead, while Redfish-based event simulation helps validate event handling and end-to-end service workflows before deployment. Together, these capabilities assess operational readiness, accelerate integration and help detect and isolate faults earlier in the development cycle.</p>
<p>Standardized GPU firmware updates help validate and securely stage updates before coordinated activation, maintain backup firmware images for recovery, enforce signed-image verification and anti-rollback protection, and provide firmware update telemetry across large AI fleets. These capabilities support faster accelerator onboarding, fewer update-related outages, and stronger fleet security.</p>
<p><strong>Key Contributions through the Open Compute Project</strong></p>
<p>The following OCP contributions and tools support these capabilities:</p>
<ul>
<li>GPU Firmware Update Requirements – Standardized update workflows, minimum security version enforcement, and pre-update validation.</li>
<li>GPU Management Interfaces Requirements – Consistent management, telemetry, and event handling across platforms.</li>
<li>GPU RAS Requirements – Standardized error reporting, fault containment, and recovery.</li>
<li>OCP RAS API – Common interfaces for exposing reliability capabilities and fault information consistently across hardware and software.</li>
<li>CPU Debug and RAS – Standardized debug, crash analysis, and diagnostics to accelerate root-cause identification and recovery.</li>
<li>Compliance Tools<strong> </strong>– Repeatable validation and interoperability testing to improve supplier readiness and deployment quality.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Microsoft’s leadership in advancing AI-assisted fleet operations&nbsp;</strong></p>
<p>Microsoft is helping advance OCP standards that bring consistency to RAS, telemetry, diagnostics, and management across multi-vendor platforms. Combined with platform integration and compliance validation, these standards establish a common foundation for reliable deployment and increasingly automated operations.</p>
<p>Fleet telemetry and operational insights build on this foundation to support three core capabilities:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Prevent:</strong> Strengthen platform validation and screening, identify defects earlier, and address risks before they disrupt customer workloads.</li>
<li><strong>Predict:</strong> Analyze telemetry and diagnostic evidence to detect emerging issues before they affect customer workloads.</li>
<li><strong>Repair:</strong> Use actionable diagnostics to automate recovery where supported and assist operators when intervention is required.</li>
</ul>
<p>Through industry collaboration, open standards, and AI-assisted operations, Microsoft and its partners are advancing toward a self-managed AI fleet—with the goal of improving service availability, restoring productive capacity, and increasing operational efficiency at scale.</p>
