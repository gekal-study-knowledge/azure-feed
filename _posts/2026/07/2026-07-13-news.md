<h2>The Bigger Picture</h2>
<p>Building an AI agent is fundamentally different from building traditional software.</p>
<p>With a website or application, teams typically design, develop, test, and release. Once deployed, the focus shifts primarily to maintenance and feature enhancements. AI agents operate differently. They don't just execute predefined instructions they interpret information, reason, and make decisions.</p>
<p>In banking, those decisions can influence customer experiences, operational efficiency, compliance outcomes, and risk management. As a result, deploying an AI agent is not the finish line; it's the beginning of an ongoing process of learning, monitoring, and improvement.</p>
<p>The lifecycle of an enterprise AI agent reflects this reality.</p>

<h2>Stage 1 —&gt; Design: Decide What It Can and Cannot Do</h2>
<p>Before writing a single line of code, answer three questions:</p>
<ol>
<li>What is this agent allowed to do?</li>
<li>What must it never do?</li>
<li>Who is accountable when something goes wrong?</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Simple example:</strong></p>
<p>A loan agent is allowed to check credit scores, apply lending policy, and recommend a decision. It is never allowed to approve a loan above $50,000 without a human sign-off. The Head of Credit Risk is accountable.</p>
<p>Design produces one critical output: the&nbsp;<strong>risk classification</strong>. A low-risk FAQ bot and a high-risk loan decisioning agent need completely different levels of testing, guardrails, and oversight. Getting this wrong at design time is expensive to fix later.</p>

<h2>Stage 2 —&gt; Build: Put the Safety Controls In, Not On</h2>
<p>Build the agent but more importantly, build the safety controls at the same time. Not afterwards.</p>
<p>The core stack:</p>
<img />
<p><strong>Simple example:</strong></p>
<p>The loan agent is built with: a PII redaction step (strip account numbers before they reach the model), a credit bureau tool, a policy lookup tool, an output checker (does the response cite a real policy?), and a Human in the loop gate (flag any decision over $25k for human review).</p>
<p>Establish the&nbsp;<strong>golden dataset</strong> during this phase: a representative set of real-world loan scenarios with SME validated expected outcomes. This serves as the ground truth for evaluating accuracy, consistency, and regression performance throughout the agent lifecycle.</p>

<h2>Stage 3 —&gt; Test: Find the Failures Before Customers Do</h2>
<p>Testing occurs in three waves:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Automated testing</strong> evaluates the agent against the golden dataset, measuring accuracy, groundedness, and safety.</li>
<li><strong>Human review</strong> brings in domain experts to assess decision quality, reasoning, and compliance.</li>
<li><strong>Red teaming</strong> stress-tests the agent with adversarial prompts to uncover vulnerabilities and safety gaps.</li>
</ul>
<p>The stage concludes with a quality gate, a formal sign-off that the agent meets the required standards. No sign-off, no deployment.</p>
<p><strong>Simple example:</strong></p>
<p>The loan agent achieves&nbsp;98% accuracy against the golden dataset. A compliance officer reviews a sample of 50 decisions and confirms that the reasoning meets requirements. During red-team testing, a vulnerability is discovered: the agent can be manipulated through instructions embedded within a PDF. The issue is addressed and remediated before deployment.</p>

<h2>Stage 4 —&gt; Deploy: Start Small, Expand Carefully</h2>
<p>Do not flip a switch and send all traffic to the new agent. Start in shadow mode.</p>
<p>Shadow mode: Agent processes requests → responses NOT shown to customers Purpose: does it behave in production like it did in test? Pilot (5%): A small slice of real customers get agent responses Watch error rates for 2 weeks Full rollout: Expand only when quality metrics stay within thresholds</p>
<p>Everything must be live before the first customer interaction: monitoring dashboards, alerting, human review queues, and a fallback plan if the agent needs to be pulled.</p>
<p><strong>Simple example:</strong></p>
<p>The loan agent goes live in shadow mode for one week. No unexpected failures. Expands to 5% of applications. Error rate stays below 0.1% for two weeks. Full rollout approved.</p>

<h2>Stage 5 —&gt; Operate: Watch Everything, Always</h2>
<p>A deployed agent is not a finished product. It is a living system. Watch five things continuously:</p>
<div class="styles_lia-table-wrapper__h6Xo9 styles_table-responsive__MW0lN"><table border="1" style="border-width: 1px;"><thead><tr><th>What to Watch</th><th>Why</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>What's coming in</td><td>Are users trying to manipulate the agent?</td></tr><tr><td>How fast it responds</td><td>Is it meeting SLA?</td></tr><tr><td>Quality of outputs</td><td>Is it still giving correct answers?</td></tr><tr><td>Guardrail trigger rates</td><td>Are more things being blocked or slipping through?</td></tr><tr><td>Business outcomes</td><td>Are loan decisions still aligned with policy?</td></tr></tbody><colgroup><col style="width: 50.00%;" /><col style="width: 50.00%;" /></colgroup></table></div>
<p>Sample a portion of live interactions and route them to human reviewers. When a reviewer corrects the agent, that correction is a training signal — collected, annotated, and fed back into the next iteration.</p>
<p><strong>Simple example:</strong></p>
<p>Three weeks after launch, monitoring shows the agent's policy compliance score has dropped from 98% to 94%. Human reviewers identify that a recent policy update was not reflected in the agent's RAG knowledge base. The team is alerted before any customers are affected.</p>

<h2>Stage 6 —&gt; Iterate: The Agent Is Never Finished</h2>

The team loops back to Design, specifies the new requirement, updates the agent in Build, re-tests against an updated golden dataset, and redeploys within three weeks.</p>

<h2>Why the Loop Is the Most Important Part</h2>
<p>Most teams focus on stages 1–4. The loop through stage 6 is what separates agents that stay safe from agents that drift into risk over time.</p>
<p>Regulators do not just ask "was it safe when you launched it?" They ask, "is it safe now and can you prove it has been improving?"</p>
<p>The iterate loop, supported by Microsoft Foundry's continuous evaluation and monitoring capabilities, is how you answer yes.</p>
<h2>Summary</h2>
<p>Design what the agent can and cannot do. Build the safety controls into the system at the same time as the agent itself. Microsoft Foundry Agent Service, Azure AI Content Safety, and Azure AI Search provide the core infrastructure. Test it in three waves before any customer sees it, using Microsoft Foundry's evaluation SDK and built-in evaluators. Deploy it carefully in stages, watching every metric through Azure Monitor and Application Insights. Once live, monitor it continuously using Microsoft Foundry's online evaluation. And when something changes a policy, a regulation, a performance drift&nbsp; loop back to the right stage and run the cycle again. The agent is never finished. The loop is the product.</p>
